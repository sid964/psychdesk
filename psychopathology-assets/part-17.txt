Duration, associated features and impairment help differentiate conditions.","A culturally accepted experience should not be classified solely by surface unfamiliarity; distress, loss of reality testing and functional impact matter.","Delusions are strongly held beliefs that remain resistant to contrary evidence and are inconsistent with available reality-based explanations, with necessary attention to the person's cultural framework. Themes can include persecution, special significance or other forms of unusual conviction. The chapter considers delusions as one part of a broader syndrome and cautions against inferring that disagreement with the assessor is itself pathological. Assessment asks how the belief arose, whether it is fixed, what evidence the person considers and how behavior is affected. Cognitive and social processes may contribute to interpretation while biological vulnerability remains relevant.","Unpopular opinions and culturally shared beliefs are not automatically delusions. Rigidity, implausibility and functional impact must be evaluated together.","Fear, mistrust and personal significance may intensify the experience, affecting avoidance and interactions with others.","Hallucinations are perception-like experiences occurring without corresponding external stimulation, often experienced vividly and involuntarily. The textbook discusses their relevance to psychotic disorders while recognizing that similar experiences can accompany sleep transitions, substances, sensory conditions and other disorders. Auditory experiences are especially prominent in schizophrenia, but their content, distress and behavioral influence vary. A clinician asks about context, controllability, meaning and risk rather than assuming that all hallucinations have identical causes. Psychological distress and interpersonal responses can modify how a person copes with an experience even when the perception persists.","Hearing a voice and believing the voice has a particular authority are different processes that may need different therapeutic attention.","Sleep-related perceptions, intoxication and medical conditions can produce unusual experiences without establishing a primary schizophrenia-spectrum disorder.","Negative symptoms involve reductions in functions usually present, such as motivation, speech, pleasure, social engagement or emotional expressiveness. The chapter describes avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality and reduced affective expression. These features can contribute substantially to long-term disability even when hallucinations or delusions are well controlled. Their assessment is complicated because depression, medication effects, environmental deprivation and other problems may resemble primary negative symptoms. A person appearing unmotivated should not be judged as simply refusing to try; changes in goal-directed activity may be integral to the disorder or influenced by other treatable conditions.","Reduced movement or speech might reflect a core negative process, medication-related effects, depression or fear of social situations. Similar outward signs do not establish the same mechanism.","Reduced ability to begin tasks or sustain relationships can impair education and work even without dramatic positive symptoms.","Disorganized speech reflects difficulties maintaining coherent, goal-directed communication. The person may shift between loosely related ideas, respond tangentially or use language in ways that make intended meaning difficult to follow. The chapter distinguishes such formal thought disturbances from ordinary creativity, language difference or unfamiliar metaphor. Assessment considers how persistent the pattern is, whether it fluctuates with stress or substances, and how it affects practical communication. Disorganization can accompany broader difficulties in attention and executive functioning and should not be viewed only as a language problem.","A person may communicate an unusual belief clearly or express an ordinary idea with highly disorganized structure. These are separate clinical observations.","Speech that seems unusual to an unfamiliar examiner may reflect dialect, translation or a different conversational style rather than psychopathology.","The chapter distinguishes schizophrenia from other disorders involving psychosis, including schizophreniform disorder, schizoaffective disorder, delusional disorder and brief psychotic disorder. Duration, associated mood episodes, the range of symptoms and functional course help differentiate these categories. These distinctions matter for research samples and treatment planning, but their boundaries can be difficult when symptoms change over time. A single cross-sectional assessment may not capture the history required for accurate classification. Clinical reasoning should also consider substance-induced and medical explanations before assigning a primary psychotic disorder.","The relationship between psychotic and mood symptoms across the illness course is central to classification, not simply their presence on one day.","A short psychotic episode and an enduring syndrome may share some symptoms while carrying different diagnostic implications and follow-up needs.","Avolition refers to reduced initiation and persistence of goal-directed activity. It can look like laziness from the outside, but the clinical concern is a sustained difficulty getting started and following through on meaningful tasks. In schizophrenia it is considered a negative symptom, though clinicians must also consider depression, medication effects, environmental deprivation and fatigue. Appropriate support depends on identifying these different possibilities.","A person may still experience pleasure in some situations while struggling to initiate activities or plan steps toward them.","Reduced engagement in self-care, study or relationships helps establish the functional significance of diminished goal-directed behavior.","Alogia is a reduction in the quantity or informative content of speech. Responses may be brief, less spontaneous or contain limited elaboration, but the observation requires a careful interview rather than judging speech from a single quiet encounter. Limited speech can also reflect language barriers, anxiety, depression, medication or a person's typical communication style. The distinction matters because alogia belongs to the negative-symptom domain rather than being simply disorganized speech.","A short response may represent reduced verbal output, while disorganized speech concerns coherence and the relationships between ideas.","Differences in language and social comfort must be considered before identifying a persistent clinical symptom.","Anhedonia concerns diminished experience of or response to pleasurable activities, but research distinguishes anticipating pleasure from enjoyment during an experience. The chapter's negative-symptom discussion shows that a person may have difficulty planning rewarding activities even when some in-the-moment enjoyment remains. Clinicians also consider depression and environmental deprivation when interpreting reduced interest or pleasure.","Expecting, seeking and enjoying an activity are related processes but do not have to be equally affected.","Reduced activity may arise from motivation, opportunity and cognitive difficulties as well as reduced pleasure.","Asociality describes diminished involvement in social relationships and activities. It can be related to reduced motivation or interest but should be distinguished from socially anxious avoidance, suspiciousness, lack of opportunity and isolation caused by stigma. A person's wish for relationships may not match their observed frequency of contact. Assessment therefore asks about preferences, prior functioning, living conditions and the barriers to interaction.","Limited interaction can reflect a negative symptom, fear, hostile environments or practical barriers, which suggest different supports.","Counting encounters alone does not capture satisfaction, meaningful connection or changes from the person's earlier social functioning.","Affective flattening or blunted expression involves reduced outward display of emotion through facial movement, vocal tone or gesture. It does not prove that a person lacks all inner emotion. The chapter distinguishes negative symptoms of expression from the subjective experience of feeling, and assessment must account for medication effects, culture, depression and individual communication style. An insensitive reading of limited expressiveness can increase stigma and misinterpret a person's needs.","Reduced visible response should not be equated with indifference or an inability to care about others.","Ratings need repeated observation and contextual information rather than stereotypes about how emotion ought to look.","Disorganized symptoms concern disturbances in the organization of thinking, speaking or behavior. Speech may move between loosely connected ideas or become difficult to follow; behavior may appear poorly coordinated with a situation. These difficulties are not identical to hallucinations, delusions or negative symptoms, although they can coexist. The clinician examines whether the pattern is sustained and how it affects communication, self-care and other functioning.","An unusual topic or expressive style is not sufficient; the clinical question is whether the structure of communication or action is markedly disrupted.","Substance effects, acute mood changes, neurological problems and language differences must be considered when interpreting incoherence.","Schizoaffective disorder involves psychotic symptoms together with significant mood episodes, but the timing of these experiences is critical to differentiation. It is not enough that a person with schizophrenia has ever felt depressed or that someone with bipolar disorder has had psychotic symptoms. The clinician must reconstruct periods of illness and determine how psychosis relates to mood disturbance. This longitudinal requirement helps explain why the diagnosis can be difficult to apply reliably.","Psychotic symptoms occurring only during severe mood episodes suggest a different classification from psychosis also present independently.","Information from repeated episodes and collateral sources may alter the best account of the course.","Delusional disorder involves persistent delusions without the broader pattern of disturbances required for schizophrenia. The person may remain relatively organized and capable in many areas of life outside the specific belief system. The unusual content of a belief alone is not the full assessment: cultural context, degree of conviction, response to contrary evidence and possible medical or substance explanations matter.","The presence of an organized life does not remove distress, but it changes the comparison with schizophrenia.","A strongly held belief shared within a person's cultural or religious setting should not automatically be pathologized.","Psychotic disorders involve disruptions in perception, belief, thought organization or behavior, but these domains can vary substantially between individuals. The textbook distinguishes positive symptoms such as hallucinations or delusions, negative symptoms such as reduced expression or motivation, and disorganized speech or behavior. Their combinations and course are more informative than one striking symptom. Diagnostic evaluation must also consider substance use, medical causes and mood episodes.","Two people with the same diagnosis may show very different mixes of positive, negative, cognitive and disorganized features.","Similar symptoms can occur in brief psychotic states and other conditions, so longitudinal pattern is essential.","Inappropriate affect refers to emotional expression that appears poorly matched to a situation, such as laughter during an upsetting conversation. Disorganized behavior may involve erratic actions, difficulty organizing everyday tasks or unusual motor patterns.
